The **Boost Post** button is tempting. It’s right there under every post, it takes thirty seconds, and a few hundred rupees later you see likes rolling in. But likes don’t pay rent — and for most local businesses, boosting is the most expensive way to advertise on Facebook and Instagram.

Meta’s advertising platform is capable of finding exactly the right customers for a clinic, restaurant, garage or hotel. It just needs to be set up with a clear goal. Here is the framework we use.

## Start with the offer, not the ad

Before creative, targeting or budgets, answer one question: *why should someone act now?*

A strong offer is specific and relevant to the audience:

- “Free dental check-up and X-ray for new patients this month”
- “Weekend table for four with a complimentary starter platter”
- “Doorstep car service with pickup — book in two minutes”

Weak offers (“Quality service at affordable prices”) produce weak results no matter how good the targeting is.

## Choose the right campaign objective

Meta optimises for whatever objective you choose. If you choose engagement, it finds people who like things. If you choose leads or sales, it looks for people likely to become customers.

| Your goal | Campaign objective to consider |
| --- | --- |
| Enquiries via form or WhatsApp | Leads |
| Bookings or purchases on your website | Sales (with conversion tracking) |
| Calls to your business | Leads or Traffic with call ads |
| Awareness for a launch or event | Awareness or Reach |

## Set up tracking before you spend

Without tracking, you’re guessing. At minimum:

1. Install the **Meta Pixel** on your website
2. Configure the **Conversions API** for more reliable data as browsers restrict tracking
3. Define events that matter — form submissions, bookings, WhatsApp clicks, purchases
4. Record where each enquiry came from, so you can compare ads with actual business results

This is also where your website matters. A slow, confusing landing page wastes every rupee spent on getting people there.

## Build a simple funnel

Most people don’t buy from the first ad they see. A simple three-stage structure works well for local businesses:

- **Cold audiences:** people in your area who match your ideal customer, shown your strongest offer or story
- **Warm audiences:** people who watched your videos, engaged with your profile or visited your site, shown proof — reviews, results, behind-the-scenes content
- **Hot audiences:** people who started but didn’t finish an enquiry or booking, shown a direct reminder with a clear next step

We explain funnels more broadly in [how to build a digital marketing funnel that converts](/blog/digital-marketing-funnel-that-converts).

## Test creative systematically

Creative is the biggest lever in modern Meta advertising. Instead of guessing, test variations:

- **Hooks:** the first line or first two seconds of video
- **Formats:** short vertical video, carousel, single image
- **Angles:** price, convenience, quality, social proof, urgency
- **Faces:** real team members often outperform polished stock imagery

Change one thing at a time where possible, let each test gather enough data, and keep what works.

> Your ad isn’t competing with other businesses. It’s competing with everything else in someone’s feed.

## Judge results by business outcomes

Cost per click and cost per lead are useful, but they aren’t the finish line. Ten cheap leads that never answer the phone cost more than three expensive leads who book.

Track the full journey: lead → conversation → booking → revenue. Share feedback on lead quality with whoever runs your ads, weekly. That loop is where most campaigns go from average to profitable.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Targeting too narrowly with dozens of interests on a small budget
- Changing campaigns every day before they’ve had time to learn
- Sending ad traffic to a homepage instead of a focused landing page
- Ignoring comments and messages on ads — they are live customer conversations
- Stopping after one failed ad instead of testing new angles
