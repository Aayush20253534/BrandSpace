When someone nearby searches for “dentist near me”, “best café in Civil Lines” or “car service Prayagraj”, Google usually answers before they ever reach a website. The map pack — those three businesses with stars, photos and a call button — is where a huge share of local decisions are made.

That space is controlled almost entirely by your **Google Business Profile**. It’s free, it’s powerful, and for most small businesses it is badly under-used. This is the checklist we work through for every local client at BrandSpace.

## 1. Get the fundamentals exactly right

Google trusts profiles that are complete, consistent and accurate. Start here before anything else.

- **Business name:** use your real-world name, exactly as it appears on your signage. Adding keywords (“Best Dental Clinic Prayagraj”) can get a profile suspended.
- **Primary category:** this is the single most important ranking factor you control. Choose the most specific category that describes your core business — “Cosmetic dentist” rather than just “Dentist” if that’s what you mainly do.
- **Secondary categories:** add the other services you genuinely offer, but don’t pad the list.
- **Address and service area:** make sure the map pin sits on your actual entrance. If you visit customers, set a realistic service area.
- **Phone and website:** use a local number that’s answered, and link to the most relevant page of your site — not always the homepage.
- **Hours:** keep regular and holiday hours up to date. Few things lose a customer faster than arriving at a closed door.

## 2. Keep your NAP consistent everywhere

NAP stands for name, address and phone number. Google cross-checks your details across the web — directories, social profiles, your own website. Small differences (“Rd.” vs “Road”, an old phone number on a directory) create doubt.

Audit the places you’re listed, correct anything outdated and make sure your website’s footer and contact page match your profile exactly. This is one of the quiet foundations of [local SEO](/blog/local-seo-guide-for-small-businesses).

## 3. Fill in services, products and attributes

Most profiles stop at the basics. Don’t.

- **Services:** list each service with a short, plain-language description. These help Google match you to specific searches.
- **Products:** useful for restaurants, retailers and clinics with clear packages. Include prices where you can.
- **Attributes:** wheelchair access, women-led, online appointments, free Wi-Fi — attributes appear on your profile and can influence choice.
- **Business description:** 750 characters to explain who you are, who you help and what makes you different. Write it for people, not algorithms.

## 4. Treat photos as your shop window

Profiles with good photos get noticeably more clicks, calls and direction requests. Customers want to know what to expect before they visit.

Upload a clear logo and cover photo, then add real images of your exterior (so people recognise you from the street), interior, team, and your work or products. Avoid heavy filters and stock images. Add a few new photos every month — freshness is a signal too.

> A profile is often the first impression of your business. Make sure it looks like the place customers will actually walk into.

## 5. Build a steady rhythm of reviews

Reviews influence both ranking and conversion. What matters is not only your average rating, but how many reviews you have, how recent they are and how you respond.

1. **Ask at the right moment** — right after a great experience, when satisfaction is highest.
2. **Make it easy** — share your direct review link by WhatsApp or print a QR code at the counter.
3. **Never buy or incentivise reviews** — it violates Google’s policies and customers can tell.
4. **Reply to every review** — thank people specifically, and respond calmly and helpfully to criticism. Future customers read your replies as closely as the reviews themselves.

## 6. Post updates like a publisher

Google Posts let you share offers, events and news directly on your profile. They’re underused, which makes them an easy advantage. Post at least weekly: a seasonal offer, a new service, a behind-the-scenes photo, an answer to a common question. Each post is another reason for a searcher to choose you.

## 7. Answer questions before customers ask

The Q&A section is public — anyone can ask, and anyone can answer. Seed it yourself with the questions you hear most often (“Is there parking?”, “Do you accept walk-ins?”) and answer them clearly. Check regularly for new questions so a stranger doesn’t answer on your behalf.

## 8. Connect your profile to your website

Your profile and your website should reinforce each other. Your site should mention the same services, the same location and the same contact details, and ideally include pages for your key services. Adding [LocalBusiness structured data](/blog/why-your-website-loses-customers-in-3-seconds) to your site helps search engines connect the two.

When the profile sends someone to your site, make sure the page loads fast on mobile and makes calling, booking or messaging effortless.

## 9. Measure what matters

Your profile’s performance report shows searches, views, calls, website clicks and direction requests. Track them monthly. Look for trends after you add photos, collect reviews or publish posts — and double down on what moves the numbers.
