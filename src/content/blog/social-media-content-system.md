Most small businesses start social media with enthusiasm. They post every day for three weeks, then life gets busy. Posts become occasional, then rare. The profile goes quiet, and anyone who checks it wonders whether the business is still open.

The problem is rarely effort. It’s the lack of a system. Consistency beats virality — and consistency comes from process, not motivation.

## Step 1: Define three to five content pillars

Content pillars are the recurring themes your brand talks about. They keep you focused and make planning much easier. For a restaurant, pillars might be:

1. **The food** — dishes, ingredients, specials
2. **The experience** — ambience, events, celebrations
3. **The people** — chefs, staff, regulars (with permission)
4. **Proof** — reviews, reactions, milestones
5. **Offers** — timely reasons to visit

A dental clinic might choose education, patient stories, the team, technology and appointment offers. Each pillar should connect to something your customers care about and to something your business wants to achieve.

## Step 2: Choose formats you can sustain

Not every business needs to post daily reels. Choose a mix you can produce reliably:

| Format | Best for | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short vertical video | Reach and discovery | Medium–High |
| Carousel | Education, step-by-step, before/after | Medium |
| Single image | Announcements, offers, quotes | Low |
| Stories | Daily updates, polls, behind the scenes | Low |

A realistic starting rhythm for many local businesses: two to three feed posts a week plus regular stories.

## Step 3: Plan monthly, produce in batches

Planning day by day is exhausting. Instead:

- **Plan the month** in one session: map pillars, dates, offers and events onto a calendar
- **Shoot in batches:** one focused photo and video session can cover several weeks
- **Write captions in advance**, with a clear hook in the first line
- **Schedule posts** using Meta Business Suite or a scheduling tool

Batching turns social media from a daily worry into a monthly task.

## Step 4: Make every post earn its place

Before publishing, ask:

- Would our ideal customer stop scrolling for this?
- Does it clearly look like *our* brand?
- Is there a reason to engage — save, share, comment, visit?

Good captions start strong, speak like a human and end with a simple action. Hashtags help a little; clarity and quality help a lot more.

> Content that helps, entertains or reassures your customer will always outperform content that simply announces.

## Step 5: Show up in the comments and DMs

Social media is a conversation. Reply to comments promptly, answer messages quickly and thank people who share your content. For many local businesses, Instagram DMs and WhatsApp are where enquiries actually happen — treat them like your front desk.

## Step 6: Review monthly and adjust

At the end of each month, look at what performed best — not just likes, but saves, shares, profile visits, website clicks and enquiries. Do more of what works. Retire what doesn’t. Your content system gets sharper every month.
